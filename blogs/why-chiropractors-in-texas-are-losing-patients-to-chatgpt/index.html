Why chiropractors in Texas are quietly losing patients to ChatGPT

A strong website and Google ranking may no longer be enough if your practice does not directly answer the questions potential patients are asking AI assistants.

OpikaAI Visibility & Local Healthcare

Let's imagine you run a chiropractic practice in Fort Worth. It has had good reviews, a fine website, and a decent ranking on Google for years. Someone in Fort Worth wakes up with a stiff neck, opens ChatGPT instead of Google because that's just what people do now, and asks what's going on and who can help. Your name never comes up. Not because you're not good at what you do, but because your website never actually answered the question that person just asked.

Here is what most chiropractors get wrong about this. An AI assistant isn't ranking you by how many are linked back to your site. It's looking for a direct, plain answer to the exact question someone typed. What does a chiropractor actually do? Does insurance cover it? What happens during a first visit? Is it going to hurt? If your homepage talks about your mission and your years of experience and never once answers those specific questions in plain language, the AI has nothing to hand that person in Fort Worth.

Texas makes this a little more interesting, because chiropractic care here carries some specific local questions nobody outside the state would think to ask. Does my Texas auto insurance cover chiropractic care after a car accident? Is this covered under workers' compensation for someone who hurt their back on a job site? These are exactly the kind of specific, local, slightly unusual questions that AI systems reward you for answering directly, because almost nobody bothers to.

Just do this simple test. Open your own website and ask honestly, does this page answer the actual question someone would type into ChatGPT late at night with a bad back, or does it just describe who we are? Most chiropractic websites do the latter beautifully and the former not at all.

The fix isn't complicated; it's just unglamorous. Write down every real question a new patient in Texas actually asks before they book, in their own words, and answer each one clearly on your own site. Do that honestly, and the same systems that are currently sending patients somewhere else start sending them to you instead, because you finally gave them something worth quoting.
